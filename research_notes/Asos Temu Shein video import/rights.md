# Legal, ToS and reliability constraints on importing/rehosting ASOS, Temu and SHEIN product videos (as of 2026)

Scope note for the report writer: this is a factual, sourced assessment, **not legal advice**. "Verified" findings are quoted from primary sources (official robots.txt, official Terms pages, copyright-office/WIPO material, CDN documentation). "Inference" items are reasoning applied to the specific workflow described (browser-side HTML→JSON conversion, JSON-only upload to Supabase, separate app displays product videos). Note where a source is a third-party aggregator rather than a primary source. Today's date for this research is 2026-11-19; retailer terms are dated/versioned and change frequently, so re-check at implementation time.

## Q1. What robots.txt and website Terms say about scraping, automated access, data extraction and reuse of product images/videos

### Takeaway
All three retailers prohibit automated data extraction and commercial reuse in their Terms; robots.txt is more nuanced (ASOS and SHEIN allow product pages for general agents; Temu's `User-agent: *` effectively blocks everything except `/.well-known/`, while explicitly allowlisting AI/assistant crawlers). None of the terms grants a right to rehost product images or videos; SHEIN's EU terms are the most explicit about seller ownership of product content.

**Cross-cutting legal context (obligations of the browser-side conversion tool itself)**
- UK/EU commentary: Terms prohibiting scraping are only binding if validly incorporated; "where the user is not required to click to accept terms, the terms may be deemed to be a notice rather than a binding contract, the result being that restrictions prohibiting scraping are ineffective." Enforceability is fact-specific; CJEU *Ryanair v PR Aviation* (C-30/14) upheld breach-of-terms claims where automated extraction for commercial purposes was prohibited, and database rights can also be engaged by repeated extraction of insubstantial parts. — [Eversheds Sutherland](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/united-kingdom/insights/data-scraping-intellectual-property-rights-and-risks); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/france-protecting-a-website-from-unlawful-data-scraping)
- Another firm notes that terms purporting to prevent lawful users extracting insubstantial parts of a database can be null under UK/EU database-right law, and that scraping can trigger copyright in individual images/works plus breach of confidence where access is gated. — [Eversheds Sutherland (global)](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/global/insights/data-scraping-intellectual-property-rights-and-risks); [EM Law 2023](https://emlaw.co.uk/data-scraping-navigating-the-challenges-old-and-new-part-2-intellectual-property)

### Inferences
- A browser-side tool that reads retailer HTML already loaded in the user's browser does not itself use a "bot" to fetch pages, which may reduce (not eliminate) exposure to the "automated systems"/"robot/spider" clauses. However, the tool explicitly performs "data extraction" and the output JSON is intended for further display; the commercial-use, "copy or store any significant portion", "own database", and "personal use only" clauses are the sharper risks, especially Temu §3.5(15)/§7.5 and SHEIN §6.1(3)/(4), §8.4(f).
- ASOS's "Linking to this Website" clause is comparatively favourable ('fine … as long as not-commercial, fair and legal') but "must not be framed on any other site without our permission" means an iframe/embedded-whole-page approach is not safe; linking to a product page is safer than embedding or copying media.
- Temu's explicit ban on "frame, embed, mirror, or otherwise incorporate any part of the Services … into any third-party website, application" makes a catalogue app that displays Temu-hosted video directly from Temu particularly exposed under that clause.
- robots.txt compliance is a good-faith signal but is not a licence: ASOS/SHEIN product-path silence and Temu's allowlisting of AI crawlers do not override the Terms.

### Gaps
- Whether a purely client-side conversion (no server-side fetching, no circumvention of access controls) would be treated as "automated means" under each jurisdiction's law is not authoritatively resolved by these sources; no reported case on point was found for fashion-retailer HTML→JSON tools.
- ASOS's exact current "Reviews and User Generated Content" terms were not extracted in full; if the videos are UGC/model reviews, additional contributor terms may apply.

## Q2. Who owns copyright in product videos and lifestyle imagery, and practical implications of hotlinking vs downloading/rehosting on Supabase Storage

### Takeaway
Ownership is fact-specific: the photographer/creator is the default first owner, but retailer (or, on SHEIN's marketplace, the seller) very often owns or licenses the rights. Downloading and rehosting copies to Supabase Storage is a reproduction + communication-to-the-public of someone else's work and is high-risk absent a licence; hotlinking avoids making a copy but is technically brittle and may still breach "embed/mirror" terms.

### Cited Findings
- WIPO: "The first owner of copyright to a work is generally the original creator or author of the work." Exceptions: works created by employees (economic rights may rest with/transfer automatically to the employer, depending on country) and commissioned works; "contractual agreements may override or clarify the default rules." — [WIPO Copyright FAQ](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/faq-copyright)
- WIPO SME guide: "in many countries, if a work was created by an employee in the course of their employment, then the employer automatically owns the copyright, unless otherwise agreed"; commissioned photos/ads often leave the agency owning copyright with the commissioner holding only a licence limited to the original purpose, so reuse on a new website may require fresh permission. — [WIPO, Creative Expression (pub. 918)](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/pubdocs/en/wipo-pub-918-23-en-creative-expression.pdf)
- US Copyright Office: "Generally, the author and initial copyright owner of a photograph is the person who 'shoots' or 'takes' the photo… When a work is made for hire, the employer or commissioning party is considered the author and owns the copyright." — [US Copyright Office, What Photographers Should Know about Copyright](https://www.copyright.gov/engage/photographers)
- US Copyright Office Circular 30 (2024): a work made for hire is created by an employee in regular duties or by express signed written agreement for enumerated categories; the hiring party is author and initial owner. — [Circular 30](https://copyright.gov/circs/circ30.pdf)
- WIPO Copyright Treaty Art. 8 gives authors the exclusive right of "communication to the public … including the making available to the public of their works in such a way that members of the public may access these works from a place and at a time individually chosen by them" — the right engaged by an on-demand video catalogue. — [WIPO Copyright Treaty](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/pubdocs/en/wipo_pub_226.pdf)
- Contractual allocation by retailer (verified above): ASOS "own or have permission to use the intellectual property rights in the Website and its content"; Temu Content "may be protected by copyright and/or other intellectual property laws around the world" and includes "photos, images"; SHEIN EU explicitly assigns product content ownership to "the Seller of the relevant Product, its affiliates or third parties who have licensed it to them", while non-product Site content belongs to the Operator. — [ASOS Terms](https://www.asos.com/terms-and-conditions/); [Temu Terms §7.1](https://www.temu.com/terms-of-use.html); [SHEIN EUR GCU §6.1](https://m.shein.com/eur/Terms-and-Conditions-a-399.html)
- Practical " licence to display" route: Awin states that "When an advertiser accepts a publisher into their programme, it grants the publisher permission to use their product images and logos, however only the ones that are available in the Awin Platform. This includes banners and product images in the Datafeed. If a publisher would like to take any images from the advertiser's website which are not part of the advertising material, we recommend seeking the advertiser's consent, or otherwise, it may violate the advertiser's intellectual and property rights." — [Awin FAQ](https://success.awin.com/s/article/Can-I-use-any-images-from-the-advertiser-s-website?language=en_US)
- No source found stating that affiliate networks license **video** or **brand campaign/lifestyle video** for rehosting; Awin feed fields cover `image_link`, `additional_image_link` and `lifestyle_image_link` (JPEG/PNG/etc., min 600×600), not video files. — [Awin enhanced feed docs](https://help.awin.com/apidocs/retail-publisher-productapidocumentation-1)

### Inferences
- For ASOS-own-brand studio videos, ASOS is likely the rights holder or licensee by employment/commission; for SHEIN marketplace listings, the **seller** is often the owner of the product imagery/video, so even permission from SHEIN may not cover seller content. For influencer/UGC videos, the creator may retain rights (or have licensed them to the retailer), so ownership must be traced per asset, not assumed.
- Downloading the video file and uploading it to Supabase Storage creates a copy and publicly communicates it; absent an express licence this is the clearest copyright-infringement exposure in the workflow. Supabase's role as storage host does not supply a licence, and a DMCA-style notice could lead to removals (ASOS publishes a DMCA agent and repeat-infringer policy; SHEIN has an IP complaint portal).
- Hotlinking the retailer CDN URL from the app avoids copying the bytes into your own storage, but it does **not** make the display licensed, and Temu's "frame, embed, mirror" clause and SHEIN's "displayed … without prior written consent" language can still apply. It also hands all availability control to the retailer.
- Storing only the video **URL** in JSON is a smaller copyright footprint than storing the media, but it is still data extracted from the site and, if the catalogue is commercial, collides with the "commercial use"/"own database" clauses.

### Gaps
- No authoritative answer found on whether a transient, browser-side extraction of a video URL (without copying the file) is a copyright "reproduction" in EU/UK/NG law; likely not for the URL string itself, but not confirmed by a source.
- Whether any ASOS/Temu/SHEIN licence agreement covers video in affiliate creative packs was not locatable in public docs (network docs reference images/banners only).

## Q3. Is hotlinking a retailer CDN video generally permitted or blocked; what measures do CDNs use and what breaks over time

### Takeaway
There is no general permission: hotlinking is a technical fact that retailers frequently prevent, and the common industry controls are Referer checks, signed/expiring URLs (with short TTL), IP/geo and User-Agent rules, CORS/CORP and embed-origin allowlists. Hotlinked media is inherently unreliable — URLs expire, signatures rotate, referer rules change, and privacy features can strip the Referer.

### Cited Findings
- AWS (2025) documents server-side and CDN-level Referer validation: requests with a non-allowlisted Referer get `403 Forbidden`, allowlisted get `200 OK`; with a CDN such as CloudFront, "validating the Referer header at the web server becomes less practical" since edge nodes serve cached copies and origin checks may never run. — [AWS Security Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/how-to-prevent-hotlinking-by-using-aws-waf-amazon-cloudfront-and-referer-checking)
- Alibaba Cloud CDN (updated Apr 2026) lists five access-control features: Referer allow/deny lists for hotlink protection; URL signing with time-limited signatures validated at edge for security-sensitive files; remote authentication; IP blacklist/whitelist; User-Agent blacklist/whitelist. — [Alibaba Cloud CDN access control](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/cdn/user-guide/access-control-overview)
- Huawei Cloud VOD (updated Mar 2026): hotlink protection returns 403 to unauthorised requests; "Because the HTTP header content can be forged, referer validation can only achieve the most basic protection, and the security is low … URL validation … key value for authentication is time-sensitive." — [Huawei Cloud VOD](https://support.huaweicloud.com/intl/en-us/bestpractice-vod/vod_10_0003.html)
- Cloudflare Stream: `requireSignedURLs` "will prevent any public links … from working"; embed codes can be restricted to allowed origin domains (with `allowedOrigins` settable via API), e.g. `*.badtortilla.com` semantics documented. — [Cloudflare Stream docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/stream/viewing-videos/securing-your-stream)
- Industry guidance (2026): "scraping or mirroring: a scraper downloads your file once and serves it from its own servers. That is a copyright problem, not a bandwidth problem. Hotlinking keeps the file on your infrastructure, so the cost recurs on every page view." Also: "Hotlinking vs CORS: Cross-Origin Resource Sharing governs whether page code can read a cross-origin response. It does not stop a browser from displaying a cross-origin image or playing a cross-origin video." Referer allowlists usually allow an empty Referer because privacy extensions/no-referrer/native apps send none; signed URLs contain expiry + HMAC token; CSP `Sec-Fetch` rules and `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: same-site` can block cross-site display. — [BlazingCDN technical blog](https://blog.blazingcdn.com/en-us/what-is-hotlinking-how-to-stop-bandwidth-theft)
- Tencent Cloud (2026): "Referer rules can deter casual embedding but headers may be absent or forged… stronger designs use expiring signed URLs or cookies, narrow CORS rules, rate controls, and CDN or edge policy. Keep origin buckets private where possible." Also warns of cache-key/authorisation mismatches and 403 diagnosis across Referer, expiry, signature mismatch, clock skew and config. — [Tencent Cloud](https://www.tencentcloud.com/techpedia/144834)
- Signed-URL failure modes: copied URLs work until expiry; if a signed URL appears in HTML/JS/API responses it can be scraped and replayed; loose rules (long expiry, no IP/user binding) "functionally behave like public links". — [FastPix](http://fastpix.com/blog/how-to-protect-video-content-with-signed-urls)
- Developer commentary specific to Temu (2026): "page scraping has no documented consumer API contract: markup, embedded data … anti-bot controls, and page behavior can change without notice. A browser script that happens to work today is not evidence that the calls are [stable]." — [rottenwifi.com](https://rottenwifi.com/how-to-scrape-temu-data-with-an-api-the-authorized-way/)

### Inferences
- A saved retailer video URL in JSON is best treated as ephemeral: it may 403 because the app's origin is not on a Referer/allowedOrigins allowlist, because a signed token expired (often minutes-to-hours, sometimes a session), because geo/IP rules reject the viewer's network (relevant for Nigeria/EU visitors), or because the retailer republished/removed the asset. Silent breakage and density of 403s are likely over months.
- Downloading into Supabase Storage fixes availability but converts a technical problem into a clear copyright/takedown problem and transfers bandwidth/storage cost to the project. It also means storing third-party copyrighted media in the project's own bucket, which increases DMCA/NDPC/GDPR notice exposure.
- CORS is not the blocker for `<video>` display; the blocker is CDN access policy and origin/referer rules. Setting permissive CORS on your own app will not make a retailer CDN allow you.

### Gaps
- No public documentation found confirming the exact hotlink-protection configuration of ASOS's, Temu's or SHEIN's current video CDNs (e.g. whether `assets.asosservices.com`/Temu/SHEIN CDNs use Referer checks or signed URLs on which paths). Direct verification would require testing from the app's origin; not done here.
- No evidence found that any of the three retailers publishes a public "you may hotlink our media" policy.

## Q4. Official/licensed alternatives: affiliate programmes, feeds, press kits, licensed UGC

### Takeaway
Affiliate programmes are the most practical legal route because accepting a publisher grants an explicit licence to use feed-supplied images and to deep-link, but they provide **product data and images/banners — not generally video files or a public cataloguing API**; terms (approved territories, cookies, approval) constrain use. Temu also offers a DSA research data portal, explicitly not for commercial products.

### Cited Findings

**Awin (ASOS, SHEIN and others)**
- Awin product feeds carry "Product names, Prices, Availability, Descriptions, Images, Attributes like color, size, brand"; publishers can "browse item specifications and filter" and use feeds to "dynamically update product listings". — [Awin product feed intro](https://help.awin.com/docs/introduction-1)
- Feed fields include `image_link` (required), `additional_image_link` (up to 10) and `lifestyle_image_link` (real-world/model imagery, min 600×600); enhanced feeds are JSONL via `api.awin.com/publishers/{ID}/awinfeeds/download/...` (limit 5 requests/min, no concurrent requests to same advertiser feed). — [Awin enhanced feed API](https://help.awin.com/apidocs/retail-publisher-productapidocumentation-1)
- Feed delivery: CSV only via endpoint; up to 10 feeds/programme, 1M products/feed; automatic refresh up to four times daily — "Conversion rates drop if a price listed via a feed doesn't match that on the site itself." — [Awin feed FAQ](https://success.awin.com/s/article/Product-Feed-Download-Formats-FAQ?language=en_US); [Awin feed management](https://help.awin.com/docs/managing-your-feed)

## Q5. Data protection/privacy when URLs or product data are stored in Supabase, and Nigeria/EU jurisdiction concerns

### Takeaway
Product-only metadata (SKU, title, price, static CDN URLs) is generally not personal data; the privacy risk arises if the JSON captures reviewer/UGC identifiers, IP/session data, models identifiable as individuals, or if end-user analytics are added. GDPR (EU users) and the NDPA 2023 (Nigerian users, and extraterritorially) impose similar principles; storing data in Supabase triggers controller/processor and security duties.

### Cited Findings
- GDPR principles (Art. 5) — lawfulness/fairness/transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity/confidentiality, accountability — are the benchmark for EU data; the EU also has an explicit right of access to data under DSA Art. 40(12) referenced in SHEIN's terms. — [SHEIN EUR GCU §6.1(6)](https://m.shein.com/eur/Terms-and-Conditions-a-399.html) (DSA reference); general GDPR principles are well-established in law but no dedicated GDPR article page was fetched here — see Gaps.
- Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 §24(1): personal data must be processed "in a fair, lawful and transparent manner"; "collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes"; "adequate, relevant, and limited to the minimum necessary"; "retained for not longer than is necessary"; "accurate, complete, not misleading"; and processed with "appropriate security … including protection against unauthorised or unlawful processing, access, loss, destruction, damage, or any form of data breach." §24(3) imposes a "duty of care" and accountability. — [NDPA 2023 official gazette](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf)
- NDPA scope: applies where the organisation is "domiciled in, resident in, or operating in Nigeria", where processing occurs in Nigeria, or where it processes personal data of a data subject in Nigeria even if based abroad (§2). — [NDPC FAQs](https://ndpc.gov.ng/faqs/)
- NDPC GAID 2025 lists eight obligatory principles including "Fairness, lawfulness and transparency; Purpose limitation; Data minimisation and ethics; Storage limitation; Data accuracy; Data confidentiality, integrity and availability; Accountability; Duty of care", and warns that behavioural ad matching without explicit consent is not compliant with "specified, explicit and legitimate purposes". — [NDPC GAID 2025](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf)
- Practical compliance summary (secondary, 2026): need a lawful basis per use; burden of proof for consent; breach notification to NDPC within 72 hours; larger organisations register and file annual returns. — [Izini NDPA guide (secondary)](https://www.izini.ng/nigeria-data-protection-act)
- GDPR analogues in the NDPA: six lawful bases including legitimate interests; transparent privacy notices; data-subject rights (access, correction/deletion, restriction, portability, object, no solely automated decisions). — [KPMG NDPA analysis](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ng/pdf/the-nigeria-data-protection-act-2023.pdf); [law-journal summary](https://www.lawjournals.net/assets/archives/2025/vol7issue3/7095.pdf)
- Temu's own DSA research portal terms require GDPR compliance and "appropriate technical and organisational measures", and prohibit attempting to identify individuals in the data. — [Temu Data Portal Terms](https://partner-eu.temu.com/temu_data_access_portal_terms_of_use)
- Data-scraping commentary: "The automated extraction of data from a database does not necessarily involve the processing of individuals' personal data", but scraping can still engage privacy duties where personal data is collected (e.g. CNIL enforcement against a coupon site). — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/france-protecting-a-website-from-unlawful-data-scraping)

### Inferences
- A JSON record containing only retailer SKU, title, price, category and a media URL is unlikely to be personal data. Risk rises if the extractor captures reviews with usernames, seller names that identify sole traders, model/influencer names from UGC, or if the app stores visitor IP/analytics in the same Supabase project. Then GDPR/NDPA duties (lawful basis, notice, minimisation, retention/deletion, security, rights handling) attach.
- If the app is used from the EU and Nigeria, both regimes can apply simultaneously; designing to the stricter common denominator (minimise, document purpose, short retention, access controls, deletion path, no behavioural ad matching without consent) is the sensible baseline.
- Storing only URLs (not media) reduces data volume and retention exposure but does not by itself resolve IP/ToS issues.

### Gaps
- No dedicated GDPR primary-source page was fetched; GDPR specifics are inferred from well-known law and from the DSA/NDPA sources. The report writer should cite an official EUR-Lex/EDPB source if one is available from other researchers.
- No source on Supabase-specific DPA/sub-processor locations or Nigeria/EU data residency was obtained; verify with Supabase's current DPA if data residency is a requirement.

## Q6. Practical risk-ranked recommendation and what the tool should document

### Takeaway
Lowest-risk and most durable: use licensed affiliate product feeds (with their permissioned image/lifestyle-image URLs) plus deep links, and store only the fields the feed provides; treat any retailer-hosted video URL as ephemeral, optional metadata, and never download/rehost retailer video without an express written licence.

### Inferences / recommendations (ranked)
1. **Lowest risk — official affiliate feed + deep link (ASOS via Awin/FlexOffers; SHEIN via Awin/CJ/in-app; Temu via its affiliate portal).** You get permissioned product data/images, a commercial relationship, and stable-ish APIs; cost is free to join and commission-based, with programme approval/territory/brand rules to obey. This aligns with Awin's explicit licence for feed images and with each retailer's actual business model. — [Awin feed licence FAQ](https://success.awin.com/s/article/Can-I-use-any-images-from-the-advertiser-s-website?language=en_US); [SHEIN Awin](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/15920); [Temu affiliate FAQ](https://www.temu.com/affiliate_question.html)
2. **Medium risk — read-only URL catalogue with attribution and no media bytes.** If the workflow must convert browser HTML→JSON, limit it to non-personal product metadata + links, avoid copying photos/videos, avoid framing/embedding whole pages, show clear attribution, and make the app non-commercial or get express permission. ASOS's linking clause explicitly permits fair, legal, non-commercial links; Temu/SHEIN are stricter about embedding/copying. Expect Temu URLs to be unstable. — [ASOS Terms](https://www.asos.com/terms-and-conditions/); [Temu Terms §3.5(14)](https://www.temu.com/terms-of-use.html); [SHEIN US Terms §6.3](https://m.shein.com/us/Terms-and-Conditions-a-399.html?lang=us)
3. **High risk — hotlinking retailer CDN video in the app.** No copy is made, so copyright exposure is lower than rehosting, but the retailer's access controls (Referer/signed URL/allowed origins) can block it at any time and the "embed/mirror" terms may be breached. Treat as best-effort with graceful fallback (poster image or "View on retailer" link) and expect breakage. — [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/how-to-prevent-hotlinking-by-using-aws-waf-amazon-cloudfront-and-referer-checking); [Cloudflare Stream](https://developers.cloudflare.com/stream/viewing-videos/securing-your-stream)
4. **Highest risk — downloading videos/images and rehosting on Supabase Storage.** This is a copy plus public communication of third-party copyrighted works with no licence in any source reviewed, and directly contradicts ASOS/Temu/SHEIN terms; it also creates takedown and data-breach surface. Do not do this without written permission from the rights holder (for SHEIN marketplace items, likely the seller). — [WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/faq-copyright); [ASOS Terms](https://www.asos.com/terms-and-conditions/); [Temu Terms §7.1](https://www.temu.com/terms-of-use.html); [SHEIN EUR GCU §6.1](https://m.shein.com/eur/Terms-and-Conditions-a-399.html)

**What the tool should document for users (in-app/README, plain language):**
- Purpose and scope: what data the converter extracts (fields only), that it runs in the user's own browser and does not bypass logins, CAPTCHAs, paywalls, or access controls.
- Source terms: links to current ASOS/Temu/SHEIN Terms and robots.txt; a notice that these sites prohibit automated extraction and commercial reuse of content, and that users are responsible for their use.
- No media bytes: state that the tool does not download/store retailer photos or videos; JSON contains only metadata/links; video display (if any) is a best-effort link to the retailer and may stop working.
- Rights: state that product imagery/video remains the property of the retailer, seller, brand or creator; no licence is granted by the tool; users must obtain permission for any reuse or rehosting.
- Preferred path: encourage joining official affiliate programmes and using licensed feeds/creatives; explain that feed licences are limited to supplied assets and that video may not be included.
- Privacy: disclose exactly what is stored in Supabase, that only non-personal product data should be uploaded, retention/deletion behaviour, and point to GDPR/NDPA duties if personal data or EU/Nigerian end users are involved.
- Operational reliability: warn that HTML structure, URLs, signed tokens and CDN rules can change without notice; include a takedown/contact process and a way to remove records quickly.
- Disclaimer: "informational, not legal advice; check the retailer's current terms and get advice before commercial use."

### Gaps
- A definitive legal opinion on the specific client-side workflow was not found; this note is a risk assessment, not a legal determination.
- No verified pricing/cost data for brand press kits or licensed UGC video platforms covering these retailers; no official "video licence" product from any of the three was located.
